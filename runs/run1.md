## Snapshot

Sarvottam Cables Ltd (SRVCABLE) is an Indian manufacturer of power cables, wires, and conductors [S3]. For Q1 FY27 (April-June 2026), the company reported a revenue of ₹1,248 crore and a net profit of ₹82 crore [S3]. The company is expanding its capacity with a new plant in Bharuch, Gujarat, expected to begin commercial production in Q3 FY27 [S3].

## Bull case

Sarvottam Cables is experiencing strong demand from power utilities and data-center developers, leading to a significant increase in its order book, which stood at ₹3,900 crore as of June 30, 2026, up from ₹3,150 crore a year prior [S1, S3]. The company's Managing Director, Anil Deshpande, stated that data-center cables currently account for about 9% of domestic revenue and are projected to double in two years [S2]. The company is maintaining a revenue growth guidance of 15-17% for the full year FY27 [S2].

The new Bharuch plant, which will add approximately 30% to cable capacity, is on track to start commercial production in Q3 FY27, contributing to revenue from the second half of FY27 [S2, S3]. This expansion is primarily funded through internal accruals and some term debt, with a net debt to equity ratio of 0.35 times [S2]. Exports also contributed a growing share of revenue, reaching 21% in Q1 FY27 compared to 16% in Q1 FY26 [S3].

Promoter pledging has significantly decreased, with only 4.1% of promoter holding pledged as of June 2026, down from 11.5% in June 2025 and 35% in March 2024 [S4, S8]. This reduction in pledged shares can be seen as a positive indicator for minority shareholders.

## Bear case

Despite strong revenue growth, Sarvottam Cables' operating margins contracted by 170 basis points to 11.2% in Q1 FY27 due to a sharp increase in copper prices, a key raw material [S1, S3]. While management expects to pass on these higher costs to customers over the next one to two quarters, there is a lag in price-variation clauses in about two-thirds of their contracts [S2, S3].

The company's receivable days have increased from 78 to 96 over the last year, primarily due to slower payments from two large state utility customers [S2]. While the company is monitoring this closely and has not reported any defaults, extended receivable days can impact cash flow.

Sarvottam Cables Ltd recently received a demand order for ₹46.3 crore (including interest and penalty) related to input tax credit claims for FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 [S7]. While the company intends to appeal and believes it has a strong case, this represents a potential financial liability.

## Open questions

*   **Conflicting Revenue Figures:** Business Daily Online reported Q1 FY27 revenue as ₹1,428 crore [S1], while the company's official press release stated ₹1,248 crore for the same period [S3]. The company's press release is preferred as it is an official company filing. The reason for the discrepancy in the news report is unclear.
*   **Impact of GST Demand Order:** While the company states it does not expect a material impact from the ₹46.3 crore GST demand order and intends to appeal, the ultimate outcome and potential financial implications are uncertain [S7].
*   **Sustainability of Margin Recovery:** Management expects to pass on increased copper costs to customers over the next one to two quarters [S2, S3]. The extent and timing of this recovery, and its impact on future profitability, remain to be seen.
*   **Receivable Days Trend:** The increase in receivable days from 78 to 96 due to slower payments from state utility customers is a concern [S2]. It is unclear if this trend will continue or if the company will be able to reduce its collection period.
*   **Promoter Pledging Discrepancy:** A news article from March 2024 stated promoter pledging at 35% [S4], while the NSE shareholding pattern as of June 2026 shows it at 4.1% [S8]. This indicates a significant reduction, but the reason for the high pledging in 2024 and the subsequent reduction is not fully detailed in the provided documents.
